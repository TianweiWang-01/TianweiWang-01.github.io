Tianwei Wang

I am a final-year undergraduate studying Computer Science at Washington University in St. Louis, alongside mathematics degrees through a cooperative program between Arcadia University and Jiangsu University.

I am currently a remote research intern at UC Santa Barbara, advised by Prof. Yao Qin and mentored by Ph.D. student Chengxuan Qian. I also collaborate with researchers at Stanford University on auditing the system prompts of LLM applications.

Research interests

I study agents built on large models: how they can complete tasks reliably in open, long-horizon environments with incomplete information, and truly understand the people they serve.

knowing the user → serving the user

Personal agents

How can a personal agent truly come to know the person it serves?

How personal agents can understand users faster and more accurately, including their habits, preferences and needs, and turn that understanding into better service.

Long-horizon tasks

How can agents carry out long, complex tasks correctly and reliably?

How to design harnesses, planning and execution that help agents complete long-horizon, complex tasks in digital and embodied environments, and how to make them more robust along the way.

PlanningAgent harnessRobustness
better worlds → better agents

Simulation & environments

What kinds of worlds do agents need to learn, be tested and work with others?

Building simulation environments and infrastructure in which agents, and the people working alongside them, can learn, be evaluated and interact.

SimulationInfrastructureMulti-agent
understanding failures → earning trust

Safety & trustworthiness

How can agents become safe and trustworthy enough for the real world?

Understanding where and why agents fail, making them safer and more dependable, and helping them earn trust in real-world, high-stakes applications.
